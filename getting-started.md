# Getting Started with Vegan AIP

The vegan AIP journey is challenging but rewarding. This guide helps you prepare for what's ahead.

## The Honest Truth About Vegan AIP

Let's be real: combining a vegan diet with the Autoimmune Protocol is one of the most restrictive dietary approaches you can take. Traditional AIP relies heavily on animal proteins and bone broth for nutrition and satiety. Going vegan within these constraints requires creativity, patience, and realistic expectations.

### What Makes Vegan AIP Challenging

**Limited Protein Sources**

Without eggs, legumes, nuts, seeds, or animal products, your protein options narrow significantly to:
- Coconut-based products
- Tigernuts (not actually nuts - they're root vegetables!)
- Small amounts from vegetables
- Hearts of palm and jackfruit for texture

**Eliminated Food Categories**

On vegan AIP, you eliminate:
- All grains (including "pseudo-grains" like quinoa)
- All legumes (beans, lentils, soy, peanuts)
- All nuts and seeds
- Nightshades (tomatoes, peppers, potatoes, eggplant)
- Eggs and dairy (already vegan-excluded)
- Seed-based spices (cumin, coriander, mustard, etc.)
- Coffee and alcohol

**What You CAN Eat**

- All vegetables except nightshades
- Fruits in moderation
- Coconut products (milk, oil, flour, aminos)
- Tigernut products
- Cassava and arrowroot flours
- Healthy oils (olive, avocado, coconut)
- Fresh and dried herbs
- Non-seed spices (turmeric, ginger, cinnamon, etc.)

## Why It's Worth It

Many people with autoimmune conditions report significant improvements in:
- Energy levels
- Digestive comfort
- Joint pain and inflammation
- Skin conditions
- Mental clarity

The elimination phase typically lasts 30-90 days, after which you can systematically reintroduce foods to identify your personal triggers.
